Why it is almost free: Contracts in Japan are short. Six months before a contract ends, the player can sign abroad, and the buying club pays no transfer fee. Note: Players up to age 23 carry training compensation. So the cheapest signing is a player over 23 whose contract is ending.

Israel: what to know

There is no precedent, there is a foreigner quota, and the security situation is the biggest obstacle.

  • Precedent: We found no Japanese player in Ligat Ha'Al. We found only one in Liga Leumit (2nd tier): Takuya Murayama, Hapoel Petah Tikva, 2019.
  • Foreigner quota: Up to 8. A 7th foreigner only on a contract of €200K or more, an 8th only at €500K or more. The quota for 2026-27 is not final yet.
  • An existing bridge: Neta Lavi at Gamba Osaka, Dean David in Yokohama. Japan already knows Israeli players.
  • Security: Families in Japan check the warning level of their foreign ministry. At level 3 almost no one will come. Right now most of Israel is at level 2.
  • An existing difficulty: Clubs already struggle to bring in foreigners and turn to Africa and the Balkans.
  • Required in the contract: An exit clause in case of a security escalation.

How to become an agent

Four steps. Right now there is no binding cap on the fee.

  1. Pass the FIFA agent exam: 20 questions, and you need 75% to pass. In 2026 it was held in April.
  2. Pay for the license: $600 in the first year.
  3. Register with the Israel Football Association and with the JFA in Japan.
  4. Partner with an existing Japanese agency (for example SARCLE, about 50 players) and split the fee.

Fees

The FIFA caps have been suspended since 2023 because of European courts: 3-5% of the player's pay, and up to 10% when you also represent the buying club. In practice the market sets the price, usually 5-10% of the player's pay.

If the rules return, you will not be allowed to represent both the player and the club that sells him.

How the business earns

Three sources of income. The numbers are our own calculation for year three, not measured data.

Conservative~€17K

One player a year, and only the fee on pay.

Base~€175K

2 players a year, a retainer from Maccabi, and one sell-on every two years.

Optimistic~€450K

3-4 players a year, more clubs, and two sell-ons a year.

Assumptions: pay €250K, 3-year contract, 7% fee, retainer €2,500 a month, sell-on at €1.5M with a 10% fee, costs of ~€35K a year. The calculation assumes no player returns to Japan early.

Three sources of income

  1. Fee on pay: 5-10% of pay, every year of the contract.
  2. Retainer from a club: Maccabi pays you every month as the "representative in Japan", plus a bonus for each signing.
  3. The next sale: When the player is sold from Israel to Europe. This is where the big money is.

The first player has to succeed

  • Age 24-27, so there is no training compensation
  • A regular in J1 or a star in J2, in the last year of his contract
  • A family willing to move, so he does not come back fast

Ideas to make the process better

  • Target list: All J1 and J2 players aged 23-27 whose contracts end in 2027, with more than 1,500 minutes. Wyscout covers all the leagues in Japan.
  • The university league: We found no European party that scans it in an organized way, and there your language gives you an edge.
  • A loan with an option for the first player, to lower the risk for the club.
  • An official partnership between Maccabi and a J2 club, like Feyenoord and Urawa, or Everton and Nagoya.
  • Settling-in package: Apartment, language, the Japanese community in Tel Aviv and support for the family.
  • Japanese sponsor: A Japanese player brings companies and fans from Japan, and that is a selling point for the club.
